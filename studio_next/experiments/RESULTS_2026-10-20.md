The selected concept invented an art-gallery collapse, father's hospital transfer and water-damaged phone, then used a paper notebook as an emotional rescue. None of those events came from the product brief. Product causality was unconvincing. Reviewer objected to some effects but missed the central narrative irrelevance. Structural gate also rejected namespaced owner fact IDs.

**Revised script, run 39453363427:** Two pitches; one was an action-heavy subway station, the other a handwritten revision at a desk. Director chose subway. Four shots ~25s. Example narration: "I'm trying to record the train. But it's too loud. Too much. I can't hear the words. I can't hear myself." The concept still centered on a dramatic distraction rather than an inherent notebook advantage. A notebook cannot physically eliminate station noise: that can only be communicated as an unmistakably subjective metaphor. Product photos and actual specs are unavailable. Critic output truncated after 1100 tokens; the script was correctly kept *unreviewed*, not marked approved. Observed stage times: pitches 76.92s, selection 83.44s, treatment 246.31s; critic failed after ~190s. Early model response budgets are research-time compute safeguards, not lasting creative duration/scene limits.

**Verdict:** 4B can form different symbolic ideas and write a shot treatment, but results not persuasive enough to present as a finished high-quality commercial; reviewer reliability inadequate.

## Educational knowledge Short: Why DNS resolution changes look inconsistent

**First run:** Pitch response truncated; no treatment produced.

**Revised run:** Two concepts, selected *Glass House Paradox*, 4 scenes, 38.1 planned seconds, 113 spoken words. The visual teaches cache but mistakenly portrays DNS change as an address bar changing from `myshop-old.com` to `myshop-new.com`. Changing a record's address normally affects the response to the **same queried hostname**, not the name typed into the browser. Several lines would require very fast delivery or re-editing. The model's skeptical reviewer identified three problems, particularly the wrong URL change, misleading simultaneous browser contents and unjustified geographic split.

**Verdict:** Structured completion passed, but `approved_for_human_audition=false`, `render_ready=false`. A green workflow means the model ran and the results were saved, NOT that the film concept was approved.

## Lessons, with actual architectural consequences

- **Film-language knowledge is not creative judgment.** Rich lights/camera/angles and invented human drama can obscure a weak causal relationship between subject and story. A realistic director must justify the *narrative mechanism*, not merely list impressive shot descriptions.
- **Factual grounding requires semantic evaluation.** Existing fact IDs and keyword tests do not catch an incorrect causal explanation of DNS or implied product properties.
- **Same-model 'independent' critic is not independent intelligence.** It may make useful grounded observations but can miss central flaws or fail due token truncation. A future critic may use separate model/vision verification and objective source checking, with human acceptance distinct.
- **Model scale and latency are measured, not guessed.** 4B CPU draft took minutes; full creative supervision took longer. We must compare result quality to elapsed time and memory, and offer faster local or optional stronger plugin compute rather than advertise infinite free CPU speed.
- **Don't wire unapproved plans into the renderer.** No hand-written fixes that quietly turn a failure into 'AI made this video.' Keep the existing user-approved M3/F2 sound profiles intact.
- **No fixed genre, story template, shot count or audio gaps.** Experiments used two pitch alternatives for comparison; future creative autonomy shouldn't be forced into two forever.

## Next engineering decision

Do not promote Qwen3.5-4B to the universal production Director merely because it returns valid JSON. Evaluate a stronger reference model **on the same two briefs** or a credibly film-trained checkpoint with open weights and proper licensing. Judge natural script, fact-causality, original hook and production feasibility *blindly against the rejected baseline*. Do not claim that an entire specialized framework such as CineCrew or VideoAgent is a tiny domain-specific model.
